How it works

Your purpose-led organisation needs revenue, not just donations. Here’s how we build it.

Weeks 1 – 3
See what you already have

We understand the organisation as it actually is today; your current model, customers, capabilities, assets, unmet needs, constraints and readiness. We find what’s already working, what’s getting in the way, and where there might be something worth building.

A sharp diagnosis of what’s holding you back from your next chapter, and an initial desirability, feasibility and viability analysis. Not sixty pages that end up in a drawer.

Weeks 4 – 6
Hunt for opportunities hiding inside your organisation

We look for the value hiding in plain sight. What do you know? What do you have? What do people need? Where could value be created? And, crucially, who would pay for it? We develop three possible futures; not three variations of the same safe idea.

Your Opportunity Map and the Three Futures.

Weeks 7 – 8
Back it up with market evidence

Now we make the decision. Each opportunity is tested against impact, demand, income, capability and feasibility. We look at who else is serving this need, what they charge, what they’re missing, and whether you can credibly do it better. One opportunity gets backed; because it has the strongest case for investment.

One prioritised opportunity and the reasoning a board can follow. This is often the moment you see something you couldn’t see before.

Weeks 9 – 12
Build the equipment to make it happen

Now we make it real. The offer, the customer, the value proposition, the delivery model, the pricing, the unit economics, the capability required, and the assets that make it sellable and deliverable.

The offer, built; plus the business case for taking it forward.

Week 13
Test, and iterate where needed

We put the smallest useful version of the offer in front of real buyers. A paid pilot, a pre-sale, a prototype, a customer commitment, or another test designed to generate the strongest possible evidence.

Real market evidence; not opinions about whether the idea is good.

Week 14
Take it forward, grow your impact

The final two weeks turn evidence into a decision and a practical next move, plus a 90-day roadmap built around the reality of your team, your systems and your constraints.

A clear path to putting your new offer into your customers’ hands. No wishful thinking; no “now someone needs to implement the strategy.”

Questions I always get

Answered honestly.

Isn’t this just commercialising our mission?
The value already exists; and you’re currently giving much of it away. I’m not asking you to sell your purpose; or to charge the people you serve. I’m asking a different question: who else has a problem your organisation is uniquely equipped to solve; and would pay you to solve it?
Our people can’t pay for anything.
Correct. The buyer for the new offer usually isn’t the person you serve today. That’s the point.
Won’t this pull the team away from mission work?
It will; if you bolt another business onto the organisation. It shouldn’t; if the offer is designed properly. The delivery model and pricing need to cover the real cost of delivering it before you scale. That’s part of the work we do together.
We tried a social enterprise before and it didn’t work.
Often the problem is that the social enterprise becomes a second business built around capabilities the organisation doesn’t actually have. We start somewhere else: what are you already exceptionally good at; and who else needs it?
We’ve tried consultants before and nothing stuck.
The most common thing I hear. Most consulting produces reports. You leave here with equipment; and with evidence about whether anyone will pay.
How much of this lands on my team?
A capped number of hours; written into your scope of work before we start. Decisions, expertise, and access come from you. Everything else comes from us.
What if the idea doesn’t work?
Then I’ll tell you; and that’s exactly why we test before you invest heavily in building it. You may leave with a Go, a Refine, or a Move Away; and all three are useful decisions. Before we get anywhere near a Move Away, we adapt and iterate to find a version that works. But you’re not paying me to make your original idea work. You’re paying me to find out whether it’s worth building.
