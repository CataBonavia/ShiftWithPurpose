Our approach

Better performance starts with understanding where value is created; and where it’s getting lost.

Improving a service isn’t just about fixing a process. The real opportunities often sit in the connections between different parts of your organisation. We look at the whole picture.

Your Customers

What do they need? What do they value? Where are they experiencing friction? What makes them choose you, stay with you or recommend you?

Your People

What do employees need to do their jobs well? Where are they spending time on workarounds, duplication or things that don’t add value?

Services & Operations

How do your services actually work? How do teams, processes and responsibilities connect? Where is work getting stuck or unnecessarily complicated?

Systems & Technology

Are your tools supporting the way your organisation needs to work? Or are they adding complexity to problems that haven’t been solved?

Business Performance

Where are you spending time and money without creating enough value? Where could you simplify, improve productivity or create new opportunities for growth?

We bring these perspectives together to identify where to simplify, where to invest and what needs to change.

The goal is always the same: make better use of what you have and create more value from what you do.

The rhythm that underpins all our work

We understand the problem from all angles, rethink the possibilities and, most importantly, make change happen.

Every challenge is different. We don’t arrive with a cookie-cutter process and try to make your organisation fit it. But our work has a common rhythm.

01. See clearly

We listen, observe and explore the experience from different perspectives. We look beyond what the process says should happen to understand what actually happens, and why.

→
02. Question everything

We help turn ideas into changes people can use, adopt and build on. The goal is progress that lasts, not a presentation that gets buried in the too-hard basket.

A different perspective creates a very different result.

Here are a few examples.

Complex information → practical tool

Making complex information easier to use

The brief was to create another PowerPoint explaining workplace policy. The real challenge was helping people with intellectual disabilities have meaningful conversations about their careers. We created accessible conversation cards that beat every formal process they’d tried, for under a hundred dollars.

A different format turned information that was difficult to use into a practical tool for better conversations.

Checklist → human experience

Making onboarding feel more human

A traditional onboarding checklist wasn’t creating the engagement the organisation wanted. We reimagined it with an onboarding passport, achievements and coffee stamps, making the process more personal and easier to follow. Instead of constant follow-ups, the experience itself helped people see their progress and take part.

A simpler experience reduced pressure on the team and created something people genuinely wanted to use.

Invisible work → visible solution

Making invisible work visible

A seemingly simple handover process relied on people manually passing information between teams. When that didn’t happen, someone could go without an essential service. Through interviews and observations, we uncovered where the handover was breaking down and helped introduce an immediate fix, while working towards a more connected solution.

Addressing the gap rather than expecting people to keep working around it.

Hi, I’m Catalina Bonavia, founder of SHiFT with Purpose.

It started when I was 23, working at a startup trying to solve housing problems for low-income families. We visited dozens of families and ended up designing a bathroom that could be built in 8 hours and lasts forever. What impacted me most wasn’t the product — it was the process: deeply understanding the people at the core of the challenge, co-designing based on their constraints and making it happen even though the architects said it was impossible.

I’m curious about how people think and what drives them, about how things work, and even more curious about why we keep doing things the way we do. I love getting into the detail of a problem, asking the questions others haven’t thought to ask and zooming out to spot connections that open up new possibilities.

I believe good services shouldn’t have to rely on people working around broken systems. And I believe that purpose-led organisations deserve practical, innovative ways to make their work better.
